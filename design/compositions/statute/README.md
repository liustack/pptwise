# statute

A passage of law set the way it was written.

Code: [`src/layouts/compositions/statute.tsx`](../../../src/layouts/compositions/statute.tsx). The header comment there is the contract. The scroll setting only, offered by the `scroll-quote` face.

## ink, intangible heritage public lecture sample, 2026-10

Settled on p03, the quotation page. The board and the engine render are in [faces/scroll-quote](../../faces/scroll-quote/README.md). The round's decisions are in [2026-10-07-ink](../../rounds/2026-10-07-ink/README.md).

**What it looks like.** In Chinese the passage stands upright in columns of fourteen characters read from the right, at 34px in the heading face, a short cinnabar bar beside its first column's head, its punctuation in vertical form: a comma or a full stop in the upper right of its cell, brackets and title marks in their vertical forms. A line break the author wrote starts a new column. Where it comes from (the quote's `attribution`) stands in a column of its own at the left in the taupe. In a Latin deck the passage is set across the band in the heading face, the bar at its left, the attribution under it.

**Why.** A law is quoted as it reads, and a Chinese statute reads best in the columns it would hang in.

**What it gave up.**

- Takes one `blockquote` with an attribution.
- A quote without an attribution, or a passage or an attribution past its room, sends the page back.
- Latin never stands upright letter by letter.
