ink is redrawn as a public lecture hung as a scroll. Every content page hangs between two thin edges, with the hall and the date standing upright down the right margin (the deck's `organization` and the footer's `label`, as the author writes them), the volume the page belongs to upright in cinnabar down the left margin (the page's `kicker`, now accepted on every content page), the claim in kaishu on one line across the page and broken at a comma or a colon only when it has to be, the source at the foot and the page number against the right edge. In a Latin deck those margins turn a quarter to read from the top instead of standing letter by letter. Its cover hangs a title slip beside a photograph with the title upright and a cinnabar seal (`stamp`), the subtitle, the hall with the occasion and the date in columns beside it; its quotation page stands a passage upright in columns with its source beside it; its chapter page opens each volume beside a tall painting in its mount with the volume number upright in cinnabar; and its ending signs off a clause a column with the hall, the date and the seal. Upright Chinese now sets its punctuation the way vertical type does: commas and full stops in the upper right of their cells, brackets and title marks in their vertical forms, and no column starting on a comma. Cinnabar goes to one thing a page, and nothing is slanted. The heading face is now Times New Roman with KaiTi, so figures stand on the baseline in the export.
